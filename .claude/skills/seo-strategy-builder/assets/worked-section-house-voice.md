# Version 1 — as it shipped. Do not write like this.

## Who is ahead, and on what

A Dhillon Law takes an estimated $16,432 a month in traffic that would
otherwise have to be paid for. That figure is the scale of what organic search
is worth in this market.

The useful part is not that they are ahead. It is how. We pulled every
non-branded search both firms rank in the top ten for, and two patterns do
nearly all the work.

One thing we checked that is worth your knowing: neither competitor got there on
the strength of their links. Their rankings come from how their sites are
built, not from authority you cannot match.

**Why it fails.** Four sentences withhold their subject. Three use "rather
than" or "not X but Y". No paragraph says what it is about before it starts
saying it. Nothing commits to doing anything.

# Version 2 — the over-correction. Also do not write like this.

## Two things your competitors built

**What the market is worth.** A Dhillon Law draws an estimated $16,432 a month
in traffic they would otherwise pay for.

**Pattern 1.** A page for each offence in each city. First for "impaired driving
lawyer brampton", first for "dui lawyer mississauga". Both at difficulty 0.
Same shape in nine more cities.

**Pattern 2.** Plain explanations of charges. "criminal harassment" at 1,000 a
month. "what is aggravated assault" at 600. Difficulty 0.

**What we build.** 17 city-and-offence pages, months 2–9. 4 explainers, months
2–4.

**Why it fails, and this one is subtler.** Every fault from version 1 is gone.
It is scannable, it commits to work, it names the firm. It is also not writing.

Every paragraph is a bolded label followed by stubs. "A page for each offence in
each city" has no verb. "Both at difficulty 0" has no verb. Nothing connects to
anything — no *because*, no *so*, no *which means* — so the reader is handed a
pile of facts and left to assemble the argument alone.

A real document produced this way came back at **91% labelled paragraphs and
zero connectives**, and the client who received it said it was unreadable. He
was right. A set of index cards is not a strategy.

# Version 3 — the house voice

## Two things your competitors built, and we have not

A Dhillon Law draws an estimated $16,432 a month in traffic they would
otherwise pay for, which is the size of the organic prize in criminal defence
in the GTA. We pulled every non-branded search both firms rank in the top ten
for, and two patterns account for nearly all of it. Both are patterns we can
copy.

**A page for each offence in each city.** A Dhillon Law ranks first for
"impaired driving lawyer brampton" and first for "dui lawyer mississauga", and
Ahrefs rates both at 0 out of 100 for difficulty. They repeat the same shape in
nine more cities, and Karapancev Law does it in five. These pages are easy to
rank because almost nobody has built them, so we build 17 of them between
months 2 and 9, starting with Brampton and Oshawa.

**Plain explanations of what a charge means.** A Dhillon Law holds first place
for "criminal harassment", an estimated 1,000 searches a month, and for "what is
aggravated assault" at 600. Ahrefs rates most of these at 0 for difficulty,
which means a well-written explainer can take them. We write four of these in
months 2 to 4 and the rest across the year.

Neither firm got there on the strength of their links. Both carry the same
automated junk our domain does, and A Dhillon Law's better links are mostly
paid placements on unrelated blogs. Their rankings come from how the sites are
built, so we can match them without matching their link profile — which is the
single most useful thing we learned from this comparison.

## What makes version 3 different

**Complete sentences.** Every sentence has a subject and a main verb. A
fragment belongs in a specification table — "Under 60 characters." in a meta
standards row — and nowhere else. Compare:

| Stub | Sentence |
|---|---|
| A separate set of 40, in `keywords-gmb.csv`. | A separate set of 40 keywords sits in `keywords-gmb.csv`. |
| These are the "near me" searches and the city phrases. | It holds the "near me" searches and the city phrases. |
| 86 topics in nine groups. | The 86 topics fall into nine groups. |

**Say the noun.** "A separate set of 40" leaves the reader to work out 40 of
what. Write "40 keywords". The word costs nothing and its absence costs a
re-read.

**Connect the facts.** *because*, *so*, *which means*, *so that*. Version 2 has
none of these and reads as a list; version 3 uses one in nearly every
paragraph and reads as an argument. The reader should never have to work out
why two facts are next to each other.

**Say what we will do with it.** A paragraph about a deliverable that only
describes the deliverable is half-written. "The profile answers them, not a
page" describes. "We optimize the Google Business Profile for these searches
because 'criminal lawyer near me' alone gets 1,000 a month" commits.

**Label some paragraphs, not all.** A label earns its place on a paragraph
carrying instructions a reader will come back to look up. On a paragraph making
an argument it gets in the way. Somewhere between a quarter and half of the
paragraphs in a section is right; every single one is a filing system, not a
document.

**Vary the sentence length.** Twenty-five words is a ceiling, not a target. A
section of nothing but nine-word sentences reads as a telegram. Follow a long
sentence with a short one.
