# The client-letter benchmark

*This is the voice. Read it before writing any part of a strategy document,
and match it.*

Everything below between the rules is a real strategy document the client
approved as the standard, after rejecting four of ours. It is reproduced
whole, because an excerpt loses the thing that makes it work — the rhythm of
one idea per paragraph, held for nine pages.

**Do not read this as a template to fill in.** The sections here are the ones
that client needed. Read it for how the sentences are built.

---

## Why the previous example in this folder was replaced

`worked-section-house-voice.md` showed three versions of one section and
called the third one "the house voice". The model copied it faithfully, and
the client gave up on the result in the first two sections.

That example was wrong in a way worth recording, because the error is easy to
repeat. It was written by the same hand that wrote the checker, against a
competitor's **audit** — a technical reference document for an agency
audience. Audits are looked things up in. They are dense, labelled, written
in the imperative, and that is correct for an audit.

A strategy document is a letter. Somebody reads it front to back, probably at
night, probably forwards it to a partner who was not in any of the meetings.
Those two genres want opposite things, and for a year this skill specified
the first one while the client was asking for the second.

# ▼ THE BENCHMARK BEGINS

## Introduction

Following our comprehensive SEO audit and competitor research, I've put
together a breakdown of our proposed SEO strategy for Mira Law over the next
12 months.

There is quite a bit to cover, but we wanted to provide enough information
for you to understand where the website currently stands, what opportunities
we've identified, and how we plan to position Mira Law competitively across
Toronto and the surrounding cities.

## Current SEO Performance & Opportunities

Currently, Mira Law's website is generating approximately 173 clicks per
month, with the majority coming from people searching for Mira Law directly
by name.

This means the website has very limited visibility among people who are
actively searching for services such as "Criminal Lawyer Toronto," "DUI
Lawyer Toronto," or "Sexual Assault Lawyer Toronto" without already knowing
the firm.

To put things into perspective, one of the competing firms, Daniel Brown Law,
receives an estimated 4,500 organic visits per month, traffic that would cost
approximately $23,600/month if acquired through Google Ads.

The opportunity here is to build Mira Law's organic presence so that the
website gradually begins attracting a larger share of these high-intent
searches, without paying Google for every individual click.

## Keyword Research & Targeting Strategy

Our research identified 99 relevant keywords generating approximately 20,370
monthly searches combined.

Rather than attempting to rank for every keyword at once, we'll prioritize
those with the strongest combination of search demand, ranking opportunity,
and potential value to Mira Law.

Particular emphasis will be placed on DUI, impaired driving, and sexual
assault, as these represent some of Mira Law's most valuable practice areas.

For example, "DUI Lawyer Toronto" receives approximately 2,300 monthly
searches, while "Domestic Assault Lawyer Toronto" and "Sexual Assault Lawyer
Toronto" receive approximately 600 searches each.

Our objective is to gradually position Mira Law on page one, ideally within
the top three results, for these and other high-value keywords.

## Website Optimization & Technical SEO

Before expanding the website, our first priority will be to strengthen its
existing foundation.

During the initial months, we'll optimize all 23 existing website pages,
improve their content and structure, resolve technical issues, and strengthen
internal linking.

Think of this as making sure Google properly understands what Mira Law
specializes in, which locations it serves, and which pages should appear when
someone searches for those services.

These improvements will provide the foundation for the additional SEO work
we'll be implementing throughout the year.

## Service Pages & Location-Based SEO

This will be one of the most significant components of our strategy.

We'll develop dedicated, SEO-optimized pages targeting specific legal
services and geographical locations.

For example, someone searching for "DUI Lawyer Brampton" has a different
search intent than someone searching for "Criminal Lawyer Toronto." By
creating dedicated pages for these searches, we can position Mira Law more
competitively for each opportunity.

Rather than publishing pages simply for the sake of increasing website
content, each page will be built around researched search demand and a
specific ranking opportunity.

## Content Development & SEO Authority

Google needs more than a technically optimized website to consistently rank
it for competitive legal searches.

It also needs strong, relevant content that demonstrates the firm's knowledge
of criminal law and helps answer questions potential clients are searching
for.

Our strategy includes developing 86 SEO-optimized articles throughout the
year, covering researched topics related to criminal charges, defence
options, legal procedures, and frequently asked questions.

For perspective, someone searching "What happens after a sexual assault
charge in Ontario?" may not be ready to contact a lawyer immediately, but
providing a clear and informative answer creates an opportunity for that
person to discover Mira Law.

The objective is to strengthen Mira Law's credibility with search engines
while expanding the number of searches through which potential clients can
discover the firm.

## Google Business Profile and local search

Google Maps represents another significant opportunity for Mira Law,
particularly for searches such as "Criminal Lawyer Near Me."

Currently, Mira Law has very limited visibility outside the immediate office
location.

Our objective will be to improve Mira Law's visibility across Toronto and
surrounding areas through a comprehensive Google Business Profile
optimization strategy.

This includes optimizing business information, services and service areas,
publishing Google Business Profile updates, building relevant business
citations, and strengthening the firm's local online authority.

## Ongoing SEO Management & Reporting

SEO isn't a one-time process where pages are optimized and left untouched.

Throughout the year, our team will continuously monitor keyword rankings,
review competitor activity, improve existing pages, update content, build
online authority, and identify additional opportunities.

You'll receive monthly performance reports outlining changes in rankings,
website traffic, inquiries, and the work completed.

We'll also conduct quarterly strategy reviews to evaluate our progress and
make adjustments wherever necessary.

Our focus will remain on improving search visibility for keywords that can
attract relevant potential clients, rather than simply generating traffic
that offers little business value.

## Closing Thoughts

Mira Law is still in the early stages of building its organic search
presence, which means there is substantial work ahead of us, but also
considerable opportunity.

The competing firms we've analyzed have established their visibility through
years of website development, content creation, and authority building. Our
strategy is designed to progressively close that gap by prioritizing the
opportunities that make the most sense for Mira Law.

Ultimately, our goal is to build a website that consistently attracts
potential clients searching for criminal defence services, strengthens Mira
Law's presence across its target markets, and becomes a valuable long-term
source of new business.

## The seven habits to copy

**1. One idea, one paragraph.** This is the whole thing. Almost every
paragraph above is a single sentence with white space around it. A reader
scanning a 60-page document can stop anywhere and the next idea starts
clean. Dense paragraphs are what made the old example need labels.

**2. Contract everything a person would contract.** `we'll`, `we've`,
`I've`, `isn't`, `you'll`. Twelve instances of "We will" and not one "we'll"
is the sound of a legal notice, and it is most of what the client meant by
"humans don't talk like this".

**3. Say "we'll" and the verb, never the bare verb.** `We'll optimize all 23
existing pages.` Not `Optimize all 23 pages` and not `Profile the render path
in month 4`. A bare imperative is a line from our worklist; somebody has to
be attached to the work.

**4. Round every estimate, and say that it is one.** `approximately 4,500
organic visits`, `approximately $23,600/month`, `approximately 2,300 monthly
searches`. Never `4,501` or `$23,655`. The last three digits are noise, and
a client who checks the precise version against their own analytics has
caught us being precisely wrong instead of roughly right. The word
`estimated` or `approximately` stays — that is a commitment in CLAUDE.md,
and this document keeps it while still sounding confident.

**5. Lead with the reader's situation, not ours.** `To put things into
perspective …`, `For example, someone searching …`, `Think of this as making
sure Google properly understands …`. Every technical point arrives attached
to a person doing something. No sentence above explains our methodology.

**6. Long sentences are fine when they run in one direction.** The 42-word
sentence in the introduction reads straight through because it is a list:
subject, verb, then three parallel things. Shape is what costs a re-read, not
length. The banned constructions in `../references/plain-language.md` are
still banned, and they are about shape.

**7. Name the firm, and still say "you".** `Mira Law's website`, and
`You'll receive monthly performance reports`. The old rule capped "you" at 8
per 1,000 words, which pushed writers away from the warmest sentences in the
document. The benchmark runs 3.0 and uses them naturally. Never `your
company` or `your firm` — say the name.

## What is not here, deliberately

No findings section. No sources table. No pull dates. No appendices. No
Measured/Estimated column. No competitor metrics table. No severity scores.
No Speed Index figure. No link-profile breakdown.

Every one of those was in the version the client gave up on, and he asked for
all of them out, in these words: *"We don't need to tell the client where
everything is coming from. The information we are giving to client should be
fundamentally confidence giving. We don't need appendixes. We have already
onboarded the client and this is a strategy document. There shouldn't be any
audits, complicated numbers."*

One number per point, rounded, in a sentence about what we'll do with it.
Everything else goes in the tables, or nowhere.

## Where the terse style is still right

Inside a specification block. A meta standards row reading `Under 60
characters.` is correct, and so is a schema table, a page-plan table, a
keyword table and an objectives table. Those are looked up, not read.

The rule is a boundary, not a preference: **prose is a letter, tables are a
specification.** `../references/plain-language.md` holds the table rules, and
they changed too — a table cell still has to say what a person would do, in
words the client used.
